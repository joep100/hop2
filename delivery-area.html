What we take, and how far

One size, twenty-seven Eircode areas, and a 15 km limit so nobody has to guess about an address.

The rule

Does it fit in one of your own bags? And would it survive an hour on a bicycle?

Up to 3 kg. Most of what a shop sends passes both without anybody thinking about it.

Soft things and well-packaged things are fine. The test is whether a knock would break it. A wine bottle is thick, single and made to be carried, so it travels perfectly well. A mirror, a picture frame or a wine glass is thin and wide, and it does not.

What fails is rarely the whole item, it is one part of it. A lamp is fine until you count the shade and the bulb.

Goes

Clothing and shoes. Jewellery, boxed. Prescriptions. Cosmetics, sealed. Books and stationery. Small electronics. A bottle of wine. Gifts and homeware that would survive a bag.

Does not

Mirrors and framed glass. Loose glassware. Lamps and shades. Anything that will not fit a bag, anything over 3 kg, and anything you would not hand to a customer without a box around it.

If you are making a judgement about something, ring us on 01 425 5722 before you book it. We would far rather answer that than have it arrive broken.

One thing the routing key does not tell you

An Eircode area can be a big place. D24 runs from Tallaght at 10 km out to Brittas at nearly 18, and A96 from Dún Laoghaire out to Ballybrack. So we also check the real distance: anything beyond 15 km from the city is outside the daily run.

Dalkey, Killiney, Ballybrack, Swords and Howth are all inside it. Shankill, Newcastle and Brittas are not — ring us for those and we will often still do it, just not automatically.

You never have to work any of this out. The booking screen checks the real address and tells your staff yes or no before they take a customer’s details.
